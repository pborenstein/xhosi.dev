## Positions
| Issue | Stated position |
|---|---|
| Housing | Supports using suitable Strafford County land for affordable housing and said county land should be used in the public interest. He favored having private or nonprofit organizations operate the housing rather than the county [Foster’s](https://www.fosters.com/story/news/local/2026/08/10/strafford-county-affordable-housing-dover-land/91199553007/) |
| Housing and homelessness | Supported a proposed 150-unit affordable-housing complex near the county jail, potentially serving the general public, county employees, and some people using a warming center. He said the project should proceed without new taxpayer dollars and be developed by nonprofits using grants or foundation money [Foster’s](https://www.fosters.com/story/news/local/2026/08/21/150-affordable-housing-units-proposed-strafford-county-land-near-jail/91383812007/) |
| Local development | As a Dover Planning Board leader in the 1980s, supported zoning changes for adaptive reuse of historic buildings, additional housing units, and revitalization of the Miracle Mile shopping area. He advocated leveraging private investment for revitalization and housing [City of Dover podcast](https://creators.spotify.com/pod/profile/cityofdovernh/episodes/Updating-the-Land-Use-Chapter-of-the-Master-Plan-e34ag0i) |
| Environment | Discussed the long-term cleanup of the Cochecho River and credited his father and uncle with leading efforts that contributed to the river’s restoration [City of Dover podcast](https://creators.spotify.com/pod/profile/cityofdovernh/episodes/The-Rise-and-Fall-of-Dovers-Ash-Trees-e36nded) |
